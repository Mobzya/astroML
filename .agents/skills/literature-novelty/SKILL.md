---
name: literature-novelty
description: Review microlensing and physics-guided ML primary literature, audit exact claims and identify narrow falsifiable research gaps without hallucinating originality.
---

Use before proposing new algorithm names, writing novelty claims, comparing state of the art or citing papers.

1. Search and verify primary text, DOI/arXiv identifier, publication date, published metrics, data splits and relevant problem definitions. Cite original source per claim.
2. Start with Microlensify (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19419), original MicroLIA papers/code, Roman 2026 challenge, PSPL texts, relevant noise and anomaly detectors.
3. Matrix columns: paper/year/task/cadence/data/positive labels/negative labels/noise/gap conditions/splits/baselines/results/limitations/our difference/verified link.
4. Identify 2–3 narrow gaps; spell out what evidence would DISPROVE each candidate hypothesis.
5. Combining familiar physics and standard CNN is not necessarily new. Check for adaptive ensembles, stacking, reliability gating and uncertainty-aware methods before describing novelty.
6. If no search access, produce a list of sources requiring verification and mark claims unverified. Never hallucinate citations or assert novelty.
7. Separate physical discovery from engineering benchmark performance. The project's primary contribution may be a reproducible domain-shift study if novel models fail.
